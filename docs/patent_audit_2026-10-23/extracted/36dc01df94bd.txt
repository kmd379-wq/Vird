MODULAR RETAIL SYSTEM WITH ADAPTIVE ARCHITECTURE,AI-DRIVEN PRICING, AND INTEGRATED CONSUMABLES MANAGEMENT

ABSTRACT
A robotic retail ecosystem comprising modular cabinets configurable as standalone or master-slave units. The system features active auto-leveling, a hybrid fluid station for pre-mixed dry beverages, and an AI-driven dynamic pricing engine. It employs strict pre-payment logic with abandonment penalties and an intelligent dispenser for auxiliary utensils (spoons/stirrers).
FIELD
The present disclosure relates to automated and robotic retail systems, including modular vending cabinets and master–slave cabinet clusters; climate-managed storage and controlled heating; active leveling and metrology for load-cell accuracy and gravity-feed reliability; hybrid fluid dispensing; packaging authentication; AI-driven bidirectional pricing based on internal sensing and external market data; integrated consumables dispensing; integrated waste handling; and cloud and mobile services for fleet telemetry, remote price control, and conditional remote reservation.
BACKGROUND
Automated retail and vending systems that dispense heated and temperature-sensitive products encounter technical challenges including accurate SKU identification, consistent heating without overheating, reliable gravity-feed and pusher dispensing, accurate weighing of flexible packaging, queue management when multiple orders are processed simultaneously, prevention of abandonment waste, deterrence of forced returns or reinsertion, reduction of maintenance burdens associated with liquid ingredients, and remote fleet monitoring and control.
Many systems are either fully standalone (duplicating expensive components in every cabinet) or are not scalable without significant cost. In addition, conventional pricing logic is often static or only weakly responsive to conditions, despite operator needs for bidirectional pricing—decreasing price near expiration to reduce waste and increasing price during demand surges—while preventing the dispensing of expired goods.
There remains a need for a modular retail architecture with adaptive deployment modes, active auto-leveling to improve metrology and operational reliability, controlled heating of consumables to target consumption temperatures, integrated consumables handling, and AI-driven pricing and control that can operate across fleets of cabinets.
SUMMARY
In some embodiments, a modular robotic retail system includes cabinets configurable as standalone units with independent control and payment capture, and/or as slave units coupled to a master unit that provides shared processing, payment capture, and session orchestration. In some embodiments, all units implement an active auto-leveling chassis to maintain a target horizontal alignment.
In some embodiments, the cabinet includes dual-zone climate architecture comprising a refrigerated storage vault and thermally isolated heating positions separated by partitions and optionally active thermal shutters. Heating may be performed by induction or other modalities and is controlled to reach a target consumption temperature without boiling, including for liquids heated under a sealed film.
In some embodiments, the system executes secure pre-payment immediately prior to initiating a heating cycle, manages multiple heating orders concurrently, signals readiness via slot-specific indicators, and applies abandonment logic including retrieval timing, slot locking, waste flagging, and an automatic punitive charge when a heated item is not retrieved.
In some embodiments, the system supports specialized packaging and dispensing mechanisms including a smart combo tray for selective heating, a proprietary composite vessel with patterned susceptor layers and safe-to-touch shells, a universal profiled dispensing channel (profiled guide tray) with anti-reverse pusher lock for precise heating alignment, a hybrid fluid dispensing station to reconstitute dry-additive beverages, a vibration-isolated floating smart bin for soft packaging with tapered geometry for centering, packaging authentication using digital passports and impedance analysis, an intelligent utensil dispenser, an integrated waste chute, and a mobile ecosystem for vendors and consumers including conditional reservation with walk-in priority.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION
A. Modular Architecture: Standalone and Master–Slave Modes
In some embodiments, the system comprises a plurality of cabinets configurable for different deployments. In a standalone mode, a cabinet includes an independent controller, user interface, and payment capture module, enabling local user sessions and transactions. In a slave mode, a cabinet is electronically coupled to a master unit that provides shared resources such as payment capture, higher-cost processing (e.g., AI acceleration), network connectivity, and session orchestration. A slave unit may retain local safety controls (locks, heaters, fluid interlocks, leveling actuators) while deferring policy decisions to the master unit.
In some embodiments, a single user session spans a master unit and one or more slave units, with a virtual basket maintained across the cluster. Cabinets may be adjacent, stacked, wall-integrated, or distributed and networked.
B. Dual-Zone Climate Architecture and Heating Positions
In some embodiments, the cabinet includes a refrigerated storage vault maintained at about +2°C to +4°C and one or more thermally isolated heating zones separated by partitions (e.g., metal partitions). In some embodiments, active thermal shutters manage thermal coupling between zones. Heating positions may be defined as slots, bays, or stations where an item is positioned for controlled heating.
Heating may be performed using induction coils, resistive heaters, convection, microwave, IR, or combinations. In some embodiments, liquids are heated only to a target consumption temperature under a sealed film and are not boiled. Sensors may include temperature sensors, thermal cameras, impedance sensors, and/or energy integration to control heating profiles.
C. Active Auto-Leveling Chassis 
In some embodiments, each cabinet includes an active auto-leveling chassis configured to monitor and adjust horizontal alignment in real time using a tilt sensor (inclinometer/IMU) and motorized leveling feet/actuators. The leveling subsystem may ensure: (a) metrological accuracy for load-cell readings; (b) mechanical reliability for gravity-fed shelves and spring-loaded pushers requiring near-zero incline; and (c) thermal uniformity during controlled heating of liquids under a sealed film by maintaining a level liquid surface, reducing hot spots, and improving uniform heating to a target consumption temperature without boiling or burning.
In some embodiments, the controller inhibits heating initiation and/or fluid dispensing when tilt exceeds a threshold and automatically re-levels prior to enabling those operations.
D. Secure Pre-Payment, Heating Queue Management, and Abandonment Penalties
In some embodiments, the system executes a financial transaction immediately upon user confirmation to initiate a heating cycle, securing funds before energy is consumed or product condition is altered. The system may manage multiple heating orders concurrently and signal readiness via slot-specific indicators such as flashing shelf-edge LCD strips.
Upon heating completion, the system initiates a retrieval timer (e.g., five minutes; configurable). If sensors indicate the item is not retrieved by the deadline, the system may electronically lock the slot, flag the item as waste, route it to a waste module, and apply a punitive fee (e.g., 200% of product value or other multiplier).
E. Universal Profiled Dispensing Channel (Profiled Guide Tray) and Anti-Reverse Propulsion
In some embodiments, the cabinet includes a universal profiled dispensing channel that guides containers along a profiled guide tray rather than suspending containers from a rim or hanging rail. The profiled guide tray may be defined by a cross-sectional geometry (e.g., U-shaped, rectangular, or other contour) configured to cradle and guide a container body.
In some embodiments, the tray accepts containers of variable heights/volumes sharing a common base footprint/width. The container slides along the tray while being laterally constrained by the geometry, maintaining predictable orientation relative to a heating zone, reader, and/or fluid station.
In some embodiments, propulsion is provided by a spring-loaded pusher. A linear ratchet mechanism integrated into the channel floor physically locks the container in a unified forward direction. This deters manual reinsertion and helps ensure precise positioning of the container relative to a heating zone (e.g., induction coil alignment).
F. Composite Vessel and Smart Combo Tray (Selective Heating)
In some embodiments, a composite vessel includes an inner food-safe liner, a patterned susceptor layer configured for induction coupling, and an outer insulating shell safe to touch. Different volumes may share a single footprint base width compatible with universal channels/heating stations and vary primarily in height.
In some embodiments, a smart combo tray includes active susceptor zones and passive RF-transparent zones such that a single induction session heats a main dish while a side dish remains cooler on the same tray surface.
G. Hybrid Fluid Dispensing Station and Dry Additives Strategy
In some embodiments, a hybrid fluid station dispenses boiling water for noodles/tea or hot steamed water for coffee/porridge with controlled temperature and volume. An electronic interlock releases fluid only upon verified SKU dispensing/positioning. Certain SKUs may use containers pre-loaded with dehydrated additives that reconstitute upon injection.
H. Floating Smart Bin for Soft Packaging with Shock Filtering 
In some embodiments, a floating smart bin includes an inner vessel suspended via a single-point floating mount to isolate a load cell from cabinet vibrations and door impacts. The internal compartment has a tapered, funnel-like (conical) profile narrowing toward the base, causing soft pouches to slide into a centered resting position aligned with the single-point load cell, preventing leaning against side walls. A door-slam filter may freeze processing during shock events.
I. Identification, Real-Time UI, and Slot Mapping
In some embodiments, user access is provided via QR/barcode scanning and/or biometrics. A monitor may display a map of active heating slots. When an item reaches a heating position, a shelf-edge reader identifies it and the system displays product name, nutritional data, and price for user confirmation.
J. Digital Cooking Passport, Packaging Authentication, and AI Pricing
In some embodiments, an identifier provides a digital cooking passport (product ID, target temperature, heating profile). The induction subsystem may perform impedance analysis of the susceptor pattern to verify genuineness. In some embodiments, an AI pricing module performs bidirectional pricing using internal shelf-life and demand data and external competitor market data, and expiration lockout disables dispensing for expired tags.
K. Spatial Access Control, Utensil Dispensing, Waste Handling, and Mobile Ecosystem
In some embodiments, computer vision tracks restocking activity and triggers alerts when a hand crosses into a virtual red zone. An intelligent utensil dispenser selects utensil type based on SKU and dispenses individually for hygiene. A sealed waste chute collects waste/expired goods. Vendor and consumer mobile applications support fleet telemetry, remote price control, discovery, and conditional remote reservation with an N>1 walk-in priority rule and timer-based release.
The disclosed embodiments provide concrete improvements to computerized retail systems and to hardware control, including active leveling for metrology/mechanics/thermal uniformity, sensor-driven pre-payment gating and abandonment handling, physical-and-digital packaging authentication, and AI-driven pricing constrained by safety rules and mechanical lockouts. Implementations may be distributed across cabinet controllers, master controllers, and cloud services. Data structures may include SKU records, digital passports, heating profiles, inventory counts, expiration timestamps, pricing state, transaction logs, and reservation states. AI modules may be implemented using rules, statistical models, or machine learning models constrained by policy rules.
