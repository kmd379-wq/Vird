This prior art search and summary are based on publicly available information as of the time of the search and cannot be exhaustive. Some potentially relevant patent filings are still confidential and not yet searchable (including those filed in the past 18 months), and the USPTO or other authorities may identify additional prior art or interpret the references differently than described here.
Several prior-art clusters appear close to your proposed Produce Vision System i.e. “autonomous retail for variable-density items” concept.  Below is a summary of the most relevant located patent references (attached).  
US10318976 discloses deriving an item’s volume from imaging, looking up an average weight-per-volume value, and computing an estimated weight (a core “volume → density → weight” bridge you’ll need to distinguish).  
US10586208 describes an autonomous-store smart shelf that fuses camera images with quantity sensors across shelf zones/bins to detect removals/placements and attribute actions to shoppers (overlaps your shelf instrumentation + event detection).  
US20220230216 similarly combines multiple shelf weight sensors and cameras to localize weight-change events, compare them to visual change regions, and reconcile with planograms/classifiers (overlaps your “matrix scale correction” concept).  
US10466095 and US10121121 disclose the “platform” art for multi-load-cell / instrumented shelves that infer item removal/placement and locations from load changes.  
US11501454 and US10977907 disclose cashierless-store tracking architectures using sensor arrays (including weight sensing) and learned models to associate shoppers with item events/transactions.  
Finally, US9033227 discloses checkout weight-based verification/security checks (expected vs actual weights), which an examiner could combine with the above to argue “multi-stage verification” is conventional.  
Taken together, this suggests your broad claims (autonomous store + shelves with load-cell arrays + cameras + exit/bag weighing + item attribution) will likely face §103 combinations grounded in known sensor-fusion retail systems and known “volume → weight via density” estimation. The strongest apparent patentability hooks are the specific control logic you emphasized—(i) a dynamic density calibration loop that uses customer check-scale measurements + volumetric capture to update a global density model, and (ii) conservative lower-bound density pricing that intentionally caps charges to avoid overcharging while still learning from shelf/scale feedback—because those “consumer-protective lower-bound pricing + closed-loop density recalibration” mechanics are not clearly spelled out in the closest volume → weight pricing and smart-shelf fusion art above. 
Based on this landscape, a rough estimate would be ~40–65% odds of securing some issued claims (likely narrower method/system claims tightly drafted around the calibration/pricing-update rules, triggers, thresholds, and data flow).
