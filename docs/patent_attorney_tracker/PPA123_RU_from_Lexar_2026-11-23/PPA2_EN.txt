System for Bio-Metric Inventory Management and Dynamic Freshness PricingField of the InventionThe present invention relates to automated retail systems, and more particularly to an autonomous retail ecosystem that utilizes radio-frequency identification (RFID), computer vision, biometric sensing, and artificial intelligence (AI) to manage inventory, perform dynamic pricing for perishable goods, and control access to restricted goods such as alcohol and tobacco through biometric age verification and pre-authorized security deposits.BackgroundPerishable goods displayed in retail environments commonly undergo continuous physical degradation while on the shelf, including moisture loss due to evaporation, resulting in shrinkage and quality decline over time. Conventional retail systems typically determine a purchase price using a static unit price (e.g., a fixed $/kg) applied at a point-of-sale weigh station or checkout process, without accounting for real-time biological lifecycle changes of the product after stocking. As a result, retailers may suffer revenue loss from shrinkage, increased food waste due to unsold degraded product, and customer dissatisfaction where perceived quality does not align with price.Various “smart shelf,” automated retail, and sensor-based inventory systems exist for monitoring stock levels and detecting item removal. However, many systems focus on security, inventory reconciliation, or computer-vision-based detection and do not directly address the biological problem of ongoing moisture loss and its impact on pricing. There remains a need for systems and methods that dynamically adjust the unit price of perishable products based on measured physical degradation on the shelf, including moisture-loss-driven shrinkage, while supporting both loose and packaged variable-weight goods and maintaining safeguards against fraud and inventory mixing.Brief Description of DrawingsFIG. 1 is a schematic diagram of an example system architecture for bio-metric inventory management and dynamic freshness pricing, illustrating a gravimetric smart shelf/bin with load-cell sensing for weight-based pricing, a camera configured as a visual auditor for anti-fraud and optional spoilage detection, a processor/controller, and an exit security scale for reconciliation of total weight versus charged weight.FIG. 2 is a graph illustrating an example bio-metric pricing curve in which a unit price for a perishable batch is adjusted as a non-linear function of measured moisture loss (weight-loss percentage), including a first region in which unit price decreases linearly for weight loss below a threshold and a second region in which unit price decreases according to an exponential discount multiplier for weight loss above the threshold.FIG. 3 is a logic flowchart illustrating an example dual-loop control process including (i) a continuous background monitoring loop that detects gradual evaporation-driven shrinkage and updates a freshness index and current unit price, and (ii) a transaction loop that detects sudden weight drops corresponding to item removals, verifies the removal event using vision audit, computes a charge based on exact removed weight multiplied by the current dynamic unit price, and logs the transaction for exit security verification.Detailed DescriptionDisclosed herein are systems and methods for managing perishable inventory using continuous gravimetric monitoring of product batches and dynamically adjusting the unit price of those products based on biological lifecycle changes, including moisture loss (shrinkage) while the products remain in a storage bin or shelf location. In embodiments, the system computes a freshness index and updates a current dynamic unit price ($/kg) as a function of measured batch weight loss over time. Unlike conventional systems that apply a static unit price at checkout, the disclosed system links evaporation-driven weight-loss data to automated price-per-weight adjustments, thereby accelerating sell-through as product quality declines and reducing food waste.The system may be configured to handle both (i) loose items (e.g., individual apples) and (ii) pre-packaged variable-weight items (e.g., netted potatoes or boxed tomatoes) placed in the same gravimetric storage bin.System ArchitectureReferring to FIG. 1, an example environment includes a gravimetric storage bin positioned on or integrated with one or more load cells/gravimetric sensors (e.g., strain-gauge load cells) that measure weight of the bin contents. A camera is positioned with a field of view covering the bin and associated interaction zone. An electronic shelf label or other display device may present the current dynamic unit price for the goods associated with the bin. In this architecture, the gravimetric sensors are used for pricing and weight measurement, while the camera functions as a visual auditor for sensor fusion and optionally for detecting visual spoilage (e.g., discoloration), rather than deriving weight from estimated volume.User Identification and Association (“Session Binding”).  In embodiments, the camera is further configured to perform user identification and association for interactions with the bin. Upon detection of a user approaching the bin interaction zone (e.g., by motion detection, skeletal tracking, zone-entry detection, or computer vision-based presence detection), the camera (alone or in cooperation with the processor) identifies a specific user and creates a session binding between (i) that identified user ID and (ii) a subsequent weight-change event detected at the bin. Identification may be performed using, for example, facial recognition, a QR code scan presented by the user (e.g., via a mobile app), and/or skeletal tracking or re-identification correlated to an entry token established when the user enters the store or authenticated zone. The processor stores a mapping between the user ID (or session token), the bin identifier, and a time window during which weight-change events at the bin are attributed to that user. This enables the system to determine which user removed the item(s) even when multiple individuals are present near the bin.User identification may be performed by facial recognition, QR scan, entry-token correlation, skeletal tracking, BLE/UWB proximity, or combinations thereof, and may include confidence scoring and multi-user disambiguation logic for shared interaction zones.In embodiments, the system further includes an exit security scale positioned at an exit checkpoint. The exit security scale is not necessarily a point of sale; rather, it is used to compare total physical weight of items exiting against the sum of weights previously charged to the customer (optionally subject to tolerance).A processor (local controller, edge computer, or cloud service) communicates with the gravimetric sensors, the camera, the electronic shelf label, the payment subsystem, and the exit security scale. The processor maintains per-bin and per-batch records including initial stock weight, current batch weight, dynamic price parameters, per-user session bindings, and transaction logs.Continuous Background Monitoring (“Shrinkage Engine”)The system continuously monitors total batch weight within a given bin. Upon stocking, an initial batch weight W₀ is recorded (e.g., after staff confirms a refill event and the bin stabilizes). Over time, the sensors provide periodic or continuous weight readings W(t). The processor computes weight loss attributable to shrinkage/evaporation using, for example:ΔW% = (W₀ − W(t)) / W₀ × 100%The processor classifies observed weight change patterns. As shown in FIG. 3, the system distinguishes a slow drift (continuous, gradual reduction consistent with evaporation) from a sudden drop (rapid reduction consistent with item removal).  For slow drift events, the processor computes a freshness index FI that represents product quality state. FI may be a scalar value (e.g., 0–1 or 0–100) derived from ΔW%, elapsed time, temperature/humidity sensors (optional), and/or visual spoilage indicators from the camera. The freshness index may be used to update the current dynamic unit price P(t) for that bin.In an embodiment, the processor is configured to detect a “refill event” based on a combination of gravimetric and contextual signals. For example, a refill event may be identified when the gravimetric sensors detect a sustained increase in bin weight exceeding a refill threshold (e.g., ΔW_add ≥ X grams) that persists after stabilization for a defined interval, optionally together with a staff-associated indicator such as a staff-authenticated refill action, detection of a staff identifier in the bin interaction zone (e.g., badge/QR), or a staff-only access state for the bin. The processor may further apply a time-window rule such that a weight increase occurring during a period in which the bin is designated “Locked” or “Clearance” is classified as a prohibited refill event. In such embodiments, “lowest clearance unit price” may be defined as (i) the current clearance price then in effect for that bin at the time the prohibited refill event is detected, (ii) the minimum unit price previously applied to the current batch during the clearance/discount period, or (iii) a predetermined floor price stored for the product class, and the processor automatically applies the lowest clearance unit price to all contents in the bin following the prohibited refill event, including any mixed contents, thereby creating a technical and financial disincentive to violate the zero-mixing protocol.Non-Linear Pricing Adjustment Logic (Bio-Metric Pricing Curve)Referring to FIG. 2, the system implements a non-linear pricing curve mapping moisture loss (%) (or ΔW%) to unit price ($/kg).  In one embodiment, the pricing function is defined with at least two zones:Zone 1: “Invisible Loss” (primarily water loss)If ΔW% < θ, where θ is a threshold (e.g., 2%), the processor applies a linear unit price adjustment. For example:P(t) = P₀ × (1 − k₁·ΔW%), for 0 ≤ ΔW% < θwhere P₀ is the initial unit price at stocking, and k₁ is a linear slope parameter (e.g., k₁≈1/100 when ΔW% expressed as percent). In this zone, the discount is modest and may approximate the proportional effect of water-loss-driven shrinkage while quality is still visually acceptable.Zone 2: “Visual Degradation” (shriveling/spoilage onset)If ΔW% ≥ θ (e.g., ≥2%), the processor applies an exponential discount multiplier to accelerate sales velocity as the product quality curve declines:P(t) = P₀ × (1 − k₁·θ) × exp(−k₂·(ΔW% − θ)), for ΔW% ≥ θwhere k₂ controls discount aggressiveness. Parameters may be selected such that, for example, ΔW% = 5% triggers a ~20% price drop relative to P₀ (or relative to price at θ), consistent with rapid quality decline beyond the threshold. The exponential form may be replaced by other non-linear functions (piecewise polynomial, logistic, or spline) while preserving the concept of an increased discount rate past θ.The processor may update the electronic shelf label in real-time or near-real-time to display the current unit price. The updated unit price applies prospectively to purchases of goods removed from the bin after the update.Transaction Flow (“Pay-as-you-Pick”)When a customer approaches the bin, the system detects the customer’s presence in the bin interaction zone and performs user identification to establish a user ID (or session token). The processor creates and stores a session binding associating the identified user ID with a subsequent removal event from that bin within a defined attribution window.When the customer removes an item from the bin, the gravimetric sensors detect a rapid decrease in bin weight and compute an exact delta weight Δw corresponding to the removed item(s). As shown in FIG. 3, the system records the exact delta weight, then performs a vision audit using the camera to verify the removal action and reduce fraud, such as removing items without triggering sensors, manipulating the bin, or substituting objects.Prior to the customer reaching the exit security scale, the processor links the calculated exact removed weight Δw to the identified user’s account based on the session binding (and optionally based on time synchronization between the detected removal action and the weight-change event). If multiple individuals are present near the bin, the processor may disambiguate attribution using one or more of: (i) identity confidence scores, (ii) skeletal tracking of the user’s hands/arms entering the bin region, (iii) temporal proximity between the observed removal gesture and the weight-change event, and/or (iv) assignment rules that require a minimum identification confidence threshold prior to charging.Upon successful audit/authorization, the system executes an immediate charge:• Charge = Δw × P(t)where P(t) is the current dynamic unit price for the bin at the time of removal. Payment may be performed via a pre-authenticated method associated with the customer, such as a mobile app wallet, a pre-authorized deposit/hold established at entry, or a linked credit card token. Transaction records store the charged weight, bin identifier, timestamp, price applied, and the identified user ID/session token associated with Δw.Inventory Protocol: “Zero-Mixing” / Refill InterlockTo preserve pricing accuracy and prevent mixing fresh batches with older batches undergoing shrinkage, the system enforces a refill interlock. In embodiments, when ΔW% exceeds a refill eligibility threshold or when the batch is designated “active,” the bin is marked “locked” for refill. Staff are alerted not to add inventory to the bin until the current batch is cleared or discounted to a clearance threshold. The system may “aggressively discount” remaining stock (e.g., by increasing k₂ or lowering θ) to clear the bin before unlocking refill.Refill events may be handled by: (i) staff authentication (badge/app), (ii) a refill command at a handheld device, and (iii) stabilization of weight readings after refilling. The system records a new W₀ for the new batch and resets or re-parameterizes the pricing curve.Exit Security VerificationAt an exit checkpoint, an exit security scale measures total weight of items carried by the customer. The system compares measured total exit weight against the sum of paid weights recorded for that customer session (i.e., the transaction log entries associated with the customer’s user ID/session token). A tolerance band may be applied to account for packaging variations, measurement noise, or minor post-pick moisture changes. A mismatch beyond tolerance may trigger an alert, secondary verification, or a reconciliation workflow.Implementations and VariationsLoose and packaged goods: The same bin may contain loose produce and packaged variable-weight items; pricing remains weight-based using Δw from the shelf sensors.Camera role separation: The camera is used for anti-fraud verification and optionally visual spoilage detection; pricing and charge weight are derived from gravimetric sensors rather than volumetric estimation. This separation is illustrated in FIG. 1.  Environmental sensors: Optional temperature/humidity sensors may be incorporated to improve shrinkage modeling and freshness index accuracy.Multi-bin orchestration: Multiple bins may be coordinated under a central controller that optimizes discounting to minimize waste and maximize revenue under constraints (e.g., minimum margin, clearance deadlines).Claims	1.	A perishable-inventory management system, comprising:a gravimetric storage bin configured to hold perishable goods and including one or more load cells configured to output weight measurements representative of a total batch weight in the bin;a processor in communication with the one or more load cells;a memory storing instructions that, when executed by the processor, cause the processor to:(i) store an initial stocking weight of a batch of perishable goods placed in the bin;(ii) during a background monitoring mode, compute a weight-loss percentage of the batch based on current total batch weight measurements relative to the initial stocking weight; and(iii) automatically update a current unit price for the perishable goods as a non-linear function of the weight-loss percentage, wherein the current unit price decreases as the weight-loss percentage increases.	2.	The system of claim 1, wherein the non-linear function comprises a piecewise function including a first zone in which the current unit price is reduced linearly for weight-loss percentages below a threshold and a second zone in which the current unit price is reduced according to an exponential discount multiplier for weight-loss percentages at or above the threshold.	3.	The system of claim 2, wherein the threshold is about two percent moisture loss.	4. The system of claim 1, wherein the instructions further cause the processor to distinguish between (a) slow drift weight change attributable to evaporation and (b) sudden drop weight change attributable to removal, and to update the current unit price responsive to the slow drift weight change.	6. The system of claim 6, further comprising a payment subsystem, wherein the instructions further cause the processor to automatically charge a user account using a pre-authorized payment method responsive to the removal event.	8.	The system of claim 1, wherein the bin is configured to hold both loose perishable items and packaged variable-weight perishable items, and wherein the system applies the current unit price on a per-weight basis to both loose items and packaged items removed from the bin.	9. A method of dynamically pricing perishable goods, comprising:	storing an initial stocking weight for a batch of perishable goods placed in a gravimetric bin;	continuously measuring total batch weight in the bin using one or more load cells;	computing a weight-loss percentage of the batch relative to the initial stocking weight;	updating a current unit price for the batch using a non-linear function of the weight-loss percentage that applies a linear price adjustment below a moisture-loss threshold and an exponential discount adjustment above the moisture-loss threshold; and	upon detecting removal of an item from the bin, charging a customer an amount equal to an exact removed weight multiplied by the current unit price.
